# Investment

The initial cost is the founder's time to create, use, evaluate, and maintain the approach across several owned software products. Ongoing cost includes keeping product knowledge trustworthy and resolving stale or conflicting material.

# Value mechanism

Value comes from lower context-recovery effort, fewer corrections caused by missed known context, clearer consequences before change, more consistent intent alignment, and the ability to maintain several products without repeatedly reconstructing them.

# Viability threshold

The result must be clearly and repeatedly better than asking coding agents to infer the product from available artifacts. The case fails when outcomes are only marginally better, worse, or require as much maintenance effort as they save.

# Revenue boundary

Revenue is not required to justify the initial open-source contribution. Paid complementary tooling is a deferred option if future adoption provides evidence of broader demand; it is not an accepted Business Model.
