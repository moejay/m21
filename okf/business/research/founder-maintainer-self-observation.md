# Source and observation

The project creator maintains multiple software products and uses coding agents to recover context and perform changes. After time away, the creator may be unsure how a product works or which features it contains. Coding-agent outputs can leave legacy trails that later runs interpret as current authority.

Product knowledge is encountered across Slack conversations, Linear tickets, Git-hosted specifications, Google Workspace documents, repository artifacts, and memory.

# Method and limitations

These are informal retrospective observations from one person rather than a structured study. No concrete incident log, measured baseline, frequency, severity, or counterevidence has been recorded.

The evidence supports the existence of a personal problem. It does not establish prevalence across small teams, prove that fragmentation causes most rework, or demonstrate that maintained specifications will improve outcomes.

# Next evidence

The cheapest relevant test is repeated use across several lower-risk owned products, noting context-recovery effort, misleading legacy artifacts, missed known context, correction burden, architectural understanding, and upkeep cost.
